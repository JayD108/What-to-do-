Medical signals

APALA v2

Can a network trained on other patients warn of a seizure 5–35 minutes ahead in a patient it has never seen? I built a benchmark strict enough to find out.

Seizure prediction on CHB-MIT is often reported at 90–99% accuracy. My own earlier systems reported 99.1% accuracy and AUC 0.64, and both fell to chance when I re-ran them under strict rules. So APALA v2 sets the rules first: selection rules committed before the results, nested leave-one-subject-out validation across 23 subjects, causal filters, and scoring against a random alarm with the same false-alarm rate.

On CHB-MIT, the answer is no. A six-stage search reached 13.2% sensitivity against 9.3% for a matched random alarm (p = 0.185), with 0 of 23 subjects above chance. Frozen and run once on the independent Siena hospital dataset, the model raised no alarms and predicted 0 of 39 seizures.

Two findings apply beyond this dataset. With a refractory period, the textbook Poisson chance level understates chance by 13–23 percentage points at 1–1.5 alarms per hour. And Youden-optimal thresholds keep the alarm on 58–85% of the time, so sensitivity turns into coverage.

Preprint v1.5, not peer reviewed. This is a retrospective research benchmark, not a medical device. It doesn't show that forecasting is impossible: long-term, per-patient forecasting has positive results.

Python · PyTorch · SE-ResNet-18 · MODWT wavelets · 73 test functions · SHA-256 model freeze

Medical signals

APALA v1

The model behind the live system that came first: seizure risk from scalp EEG. Formerly NeuroPulse.

It turns 686 CHB-MIT recordings with 198 annotated seizures into 590 features per window and trains a stacking ensemble on GPU. Rewriting Sample Entropy with Numba made it 158× faster.

The main finding is the gap in the chart. The model scores 0.998 on a patient it was trained on and 0.56 across patients it has never seen, barely above chance. That gap is why I built v2.

Research prototype. Not a medical device and not for clinical decisions.

Python · XGBoost · LightGBM · Random Forest · Numba

Language models

MONA

A small language model you can open up and look inside.

I trained MONA from scratch on 2.5 billion tokens, then built a terminal app that teaches how LLMs work using her real weights. You can watch a sentence become tokens, inspect attention layer by layer, debug why she repeats herself, and train a tiny model of your own. Pressing F8 on any screen shows the code behind it.

MONA writes fluent text but often states wrong facts and can't do arithmetic. She is built for learning, and the model card says so.

Extreme weather

VARUNA

Forecasts cloudbursts, thunderstorms and flash floods over the Himalayas, 2–6 hours ahead.

A 17.6M-parameter Hybrid TransUNet that fuses three inputs: IMDAA atmospheric reanalysis, INSAT-3D satellite imagery and CartoDEM terrain. The data covers Uttarakhand and Himachal Pradesh during August 2019.

Early runs were failing on timestamp misalignment and a 1:460 class imbalance. The final version replaced coarse 25 km labels with 1 km labels, which raised cloudburst signal density 461×.

Recall is above 80% for all three hazards, but false alarms are common: precision is 18% for cloudbursts and 8% for flash floods. The holdout is 11 windows from one month, so it needs multi-year data before any operational claim.

PyTorch · 3D + 2D CNNs · self-attention · NetCDF · HDF5 · GeoTIFF

Language models

MANAS

A character-level transformer trained on the poetry of Goswami Tulsidas.

I wanted to know whether a small transformer could learn the patterns of classical Awadhi verse from raw characters. I wrote it from scratch in PyTorch with no pretrained weights: 10.9M parameters, a 101-character Devanagari vocabulary and about 2.83 million characters of text, including the Ramcharitmanas and Vinay Patrika.

This is an experimental checkpoint. The output looks like Devanagari verse without being coherent poetry, and the OCR-derived corpus still has errors. A cleaned, page-verified corpus is planned for v2.

PyTorch · GPT-style decoder · MIT license
